WHY STRATEGY COMES BEFORE SKETCHING.

By Waqas AzizOctober 11, 20263 min read

Every strong logo you admire started long before the first sketch. It started with a decision about what the brand needed to say.

A logo is an answer, not a decoration

Sketching first feels productive. The pen moves, shapes appear, and it looks like design is happening. But without positioning, you are decorating, not designing. The real question is never "what looks good." It is "what should this brand mean to the people it serves." Until that is answered, every sketch is a guess.

What strategy actually looks like

Nothing fancy. Three questions, answered honestly:

  • Who is this for? Name the actual humans, not "everyone."
  • What makes it different? If the answer could describe a competitor, keep digging.
  • What should people feel in the first three seconds? Trust, energy, calm, luxury. Pick, and commit.

Then a quick look at the competitive landscape, so the new mark does not accidentally blend into the same shapes everyone else is using.

How this changes the design

Once the positioning is written down, sketching gets faster and calmer. You stop chasing trends because you have a filter for every idea: does this say what we decided it should say? Revisions drop too, because the client approved the thinking before ever seeing a drawing. The concepts stop being surprises and start being conclusions.

The sketch is the easy part to admire, but the strategy is the part that makes it work. Start there, and the logo almost designs itself. Almost.
