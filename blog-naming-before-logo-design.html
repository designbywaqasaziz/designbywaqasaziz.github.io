PICK THE NAME BEFORE YOU DESIGN THE LOGO.

By Waqas AzizNovember 9, 20264 min read

A logo cannot save a bad name. Clients come to me with a name they picked in a hurry, then ask for a logo that makes it sound premium, trustworthy, and easy to remember. That is the wrong order. The name does the heavy lifting. The logo only dresses it up. Pick the name first, and the design gets easier at every step after it.

A name has three jobs

First, it has to be memorable. Someone should be able to hear your name once and repeat it to a friend the next day. If they cannot remember it, they cannot recommend you, no matter how good your work is.

Second, it has to be easy to say and spell. If a customer has to ask how to spell it, you lose them at the search bar. Every extra letter and every unusual spelling is a small tax on every conversation about your business.

Third, it has to be ownable. A name that describes the whole category, like Best Quality Shoes, belongs to nobody. A name with a little character in it belongs to you, and that is what a logo can actually build on.

Say it out loud, then write it down

The fastest test costs nothing. Say the name out loud as if you are answering the phone. Then say it in a noisy room. Then spell it to someone who has never seen it written. If you have to explain the spelling every time, the name is working against you.

Watch out for clever spellings. Swapping a letter for a lookalike, dropping vowels, or adding an extra X feels creative on day one. In practice it means correcting people forever. Simple spelling is not boring. It is a business decision that pays off every single day.

Write the name down in plain text too. A name that only looks good in a fancy font is a weak name. It should still work typed in an email, printed on a receipt, and read aloud by a stranger.

Check it can live everywhere

Before you commit, check the practical ground. Is the domain available, or at least a sensible version of it? Are the social handles free on the platforms where your customers actually are? A name you cannot own online is a name you will fight over later.

Do a basic trademark search as well. You do not need a lawyer for the first pass, but you do need to know if another business in your field already uses the same name. Finding out after the logo, the signs, and the packaging are printed is the expensive way to learn.

If you plan to sell beyond your own country, check what the name means in other languages. A quick search now beats a rebrand later. This step takes an hour. A forced rename takes months.

Shortlist three, then let the logo follow

Narrow your list to three finalists and live with them for a week. Say each one in a full sentence. Picture it on a business card, a shop sign, and a tiny profile picture. The one that still feels right after a week is the one.

Then bring it to your designer. A good name gives the designer something real to build on: a sound, a rhythm, a meaning. That is where strong logo ideas come from. A weak name forces the designer to decorate a word that says nothing, and no amount of clever design fixes that.

Your logo will change over the years. Your name will not, or at least it should not. Spend the time on the name now, and every design decision after it gets simpler.
