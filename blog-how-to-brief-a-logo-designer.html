HOW TO BRIEF A LOGO DESIGNER.

By Waqas AzizOctober 9, 20264 min read

A good brief saves you weeks and gets you a better logo. Here is exactly what to prepare before you hire anyone.

Start with the business, not the logo

Before a single shape is discussed, a designer needs to understand the business. Prepare a short, honest summary:

  • What you sell and who buys it.
  • What makes you different from the alternatives your customers already know.
  • Competitors you admire, and ones you never want to be confused with.
  • Where the logo will live: website, packaging, signage, social profiles.

One page is plenty. Clarity beats length.

Say what you need, not what it should look like

Designers solve problems better when you describe the goal instead of prescribing the solution. "We need to look trustworthy to corporate clients" is a brief. "A blue circle with a swoosh" is a constraint. Reference images help, but always add one line about what you like in each one, or the designer will copy the wrong thing.

Be honest about budget and timeline

A real budget range gets you a real proposal with the right scope. A vague budget gets vague work and awkward conversations later. Same with timing: a launch date is useful information, not pressure. Share it early.

What happens next

Expect questions first. A good designer will push back on a thin brief, run a short discovery phase, and only then present concepts in structured rounds. That back and forth is not friction. It is the process working.

Prepare the brief well, and you will spend less, wait less, and end up with a mark that actually fits the business.
